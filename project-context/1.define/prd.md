## 1. Executive Summary

### Problem Statement

Employee onboarding is a cross-functional workflow that depends on HR, IT, compliance, the direct manager, training owners, and the new collaborator. In many organizations, this process is still coordinated through manual checklists, email threads, informal reminders, and disconnected HR or IT tools. This creates missed handoffs, inconsistent first-day readiness, avoidable rework, and a weaker new-hire experience.

The approved MRD identifies a strong market signal for this problem: HR technology is a large and growing market, onboarding remains a known quality gap, and organizations are increasingly adopting AI and agentic workflows. Gallup reports that only 12% of employees strongly agree their organization does a great job onboarding, while the MRD cites a growing dedicated onboarding software segment and a broader $94B HR technology market.

### Solution Overview

OnboardFlow AI is an AI-assisted multi-agent onboarding planner. The system receives basic employee information, validates required data, invokes specialized agents, and generates a consolidated onboarding plan containing:

- Employee profile summary.
- Missing data and pending actions.
- Required documents.
- IT access and equipment checklist.
- Training path.
- Initial onboarding agenda.
- Draft communications for the collaborator, manager, HR, and IT.
- Executive summary with risks and next actions.

The MVP will not create real users, send real emails, integrate with payroll, or perform employment decisions. It will demonstrate reliable multi-agent orchestration and produce reviewable artifacts for HR.

### Strategic Rationale

A multi-agent architecture is appropriate because onboarding naturally decomposes into specialized domains: intake, compliance, IT provisioning, training, communication, and final coordination. CrewAI is the selected runtime because the MRD identifies Crews and controlled Flows as a good match for specialist collaboration plus deterministic orchestration.

The strategic positioning is:

> AI-assisted onboarding orchestration that turns new-hire data into a complete, reviewable plan for HR, IT, managers, and the new collaborator.

## 2. Market Context & User Analysis

### Target Market

Primary initial segment:

- Small-to-mid-market organizations and innovation teams inside larger companies.
- HR, People Operations, or Talent Operations teams that coordinate onboarding manually or across lightweight tools.
- Companies where IT access, training, and compliance steps vary by role, department, seniority, location, or work mode.

Secondary future segment:

- Enterprise HR teams seeking an orchestration layer around HRIS, ITSM, IAM, LMS, and communication tools.

### User Personas

#### HR Operator / People Operations Specialist

Responsible for registering new collaborators, coordinating onboarding tasks, following up with stakeholders, and ensuring first-day readiness.

Primary needs:

- Generate a complete onboarding plan quickly.
- Detect missing information early.
- See pending actions and owners.
- Produce clear communication drafts.
- Maintain consistency across roles and departments.

#### HR Manager / People Ops Lead

Responsible for process quality, standardization, compliance posture, and onboarding experience.

Primary needs:

- Reduce manual coordination effort.
- Standardize onboarding quality.
- Audit what was generated and approved.
- Identify bottlenecks and recurring pending items.

#### Direct Manager

Responsible for team introduction, role expectations, initial meetings, and early guidance.

Primary needs:

- Receive a concise preparation checklist.
- Understand what to do before and during the collaborator's first days.
- Align expectations and training priorities.

#### IT Provisioning Owner

Responsible for accounts, devices, VPN, collaboration tools, repositories, and systems access.

Primary needs:

- Receive a clear access and equipment checklist.
- Understand deadlines tied to start date.
- Avoid ambiguous provisioning requests.

#### New Collaborator

Receives onboarding guidance, tasks, schedules, and welcome communication.

Primary needs:

- Know what to do before and during the first days.
- Understand required documents and trainings.
- Receive clear contacts for support.

## 3. Product Goals and Scope

### Product Goals

1. Enable HR to generate a complete onboarding plan from basic employee data.
2. Demonstrate visible collaboration between specialized AI agents.
3. Improve first-day readiness by identifying critical tasks, owners, deadlines, and risks.
4. Provide reviewable and editable outputs rather than autonomous execution.
5. Establish a foundation for future integrations with HRIS, ITSM, IAM, LMS, email, and chat tools.

### MVP Scope

Included:

- Manual or JSON employee intake.
- Required field validation.
- Specialist agent execution through CrewAI.
- Local catalogs or rules for documents, IT accesses, equipment, training, and messages.
- Consolidated onboarding plan.
- Draft communications.
- Pending actions and risk flags.
- Action/history log.
- Structured JSON and human-readable output.

Excluded:

- Real HRIS integration.
- Real identity or access provisioning.
- Real email or chat sending.
- Payroll, ERP, or benefits integrations.
- Digital signature workflow.
- Employee scoring, ranking, evaluation, or employment decisioning.
- Production analytics dashboard.
- Mobile application.

## 4. Technical Requirements & Architecture

### CrewAI Framework Specifications

Selected runtime: CrewAI.

Required orchestration style:

- Use a coordinator-led flow.
- Use specialist agents for domain-specific outputs.
- Use deterministic input and output schemas.
- Validate agent output before consolidation.
- Preserve action history for traceability.

Recommended architecture:

- Controlled backend workflow for intake, validation, agent execution, and final aggregation.
- CrewAI Crews for specialist collaboration.
- Flow-style sequencing for predictable task order.
- Local structured catalogs for MVP grounding.

### Core Agent Definitions

#### Onboarding Coordinator Agent

- Role: Main workflow orchestrator.
- Goal: Produce a complete and coherent onboarding plan from specialist outputs.
- Responsibilities:
  - Receive validated employee profile.
  - Trigger specialist agents.
  - Normalize and consolidate outputs.
  - Identify conflicts, gaps, and pending actions.
  - Generate the final onboarding plan.
- Tools:
  - Employee profile schema.
  - Plan output schema.
  - Local policy and catalog readers.
  - Action log writer.
- Memory:
  - Current onboarding case context.
  - Generated specialist outputs.
- Delegation:
  - Delegates to all specialist agents.

#### HR Intake Agent

- Role: Employee data validation and profile synthesis.
- Goal: Ensure required employee information is complete and usable.
- Responsibilities:
  - Validate required fields.
  - Identify missing or malformed fields.
  - Classify employment type, department, work mode, role family, and seniority.
  - Generate initial employee profile summary.
- Tools:
  - Required field schema.
  - Validation rules.
- Delegation:
  - No delegation in MVP.

#### Compliance Agent

- Role: Document, policy, and compliance requirements specialist.
- Goal: Identify required onboarding documents and compliance-related tasks.
- Responsibilities:
  - Recommend document checklist.
  - Recommend compliance and security acknowledgements.
  - Identify document-related pending actions.
  - Flag compliance risks relevant to start date or missing data.
- Tools:
  - Document catalog.
  - Compliance training catalog.
  - Policy checklist rules.
- Delegation:
  - No delegation in MVP.

#### IT Provisioning Agent

- Role: Access, account, and equipment planning specialist.
- Goal: Generate IT readiness checklist based on role, department, and work mode.
- Responsibilities:
  - Recommend corporate accounts and systems.
  - Recommend equipment needs.
  - Identify critical first-day IT tasks.
  - Draft IT provisioning request.
- Tools:
  - Access catalog.
  - Equipment catalog.
  - Department and role mapping rules.
- Delegation:
  - No delegation in MVP.

#### Training Agent

- Role: Initial learning path specialist.
- Goal: Generate role-specific onboarding training path.
- Responsibilities:
  - Recommend mandatory, institutional, technical, compliance, and security trainings.
  - Sequence trainings for the first days.
  - Identify dependencies and priorities.
- Tools:
  - Training catalog.
  - Role and department mapping rules.
- Delegation:
  - No delegation in MVP.

#### Communication Agent

- Role: Stakeholder communication specialist.
- Goal: Draft clear and audience-specific onboarding messages.
- Responsibilities:
  - Generate welcome message.
  - Generate manager preparation message.
  - Generate IT request message.
  - Generate HR summary message when needed.
  - Adapt tone by audience.
- Tools:
  - Message templates.
  - Employee profile summary.
  - Specialist outputs.
- Delegation:
  - No delegation in MVP.

## 5. Functional Requirements

### P0 — MVP Requirements

#### PRD-P0-001: New Collaborator Intake

User story: As an HR user, I want to enter new collaborator data so that the system can generate an onboarding plan.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system accepts all required employee fields.
- The system accepts optional fields when provided.
- The system supports at least one manual input path and one structured JSON input path.
- The system preserves the original submitted data in the action history.

Traceability: RF001, MRD User Journey.

#### PRD-P0-002: Required Data Validation

User story: As an HR user, I want missing fields to be identified before the plan is generated so that onboarding does not begin with incomplete data.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system validates required fields.
- Missing fields are listed clearly.
- Invalid date or email formats are flagged.
- The plan can still be generated in draft mode with pending data, but status must reflect missing information.

Traceability: RF002, RF003.

#### PRD-P0-003: Employee Profile Summary

User story: As an HR user, I want a concise profile summary so that every stakeholder understands who is being onboarded.

Acceptance criteria:

- The profile includes name, role, department, manager, start date, employment type, work mode, and seniority when available.
- The profile identifies primary onboarding needs.
- The profile is included in the final consolidated plan.

Traceability: RF003.

#### PRD-P0-004: Document Checklist Generation

User story: As an HR user, I want required documents to be identified so that I can track onboarding documentation.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system generates a required document checklist.
- Each document has a status initialized as pending unless sample status data is provided.
- Each recommendation includes a rationale or source category.
- The system flags missing critical documents as pending actions.

Traceability: RF004, RF005.

#### PRD-P0-005: IT Access and Equipment Checklist

User story: As an IT owner, I want a clear provisioning checklist so that required access and equipment are ready before the start date.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system recommends access needs based on role, department, work mode, and seniority when available.
- The system recommends equipment needs.
- Critical first-day IT tasks are marked.
- The system generates an IT communication draft.

Traceability: RF006, RF007, RF013, RF016.

#### PRD-P0-006: Training Path Generation

User story: As an HR user, I want a role-specific training path so that the collaborator has an initial learning sequence.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system generates mandatory and recommended trainings.
- Trainings are classified by category.
- The system includes at least compliance, security, institutional, and role/department-specific items where applicable.
- Training items include suggested order or timing.

Traceability: RF008, RF009.

#### PRD-P0-007: Communication Drafts

User story: As an HR user, I want generated communication drafts so that stakeholders receive consistent onboarding information.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system generates a welcome message for the collaborator.
- The system generates a manager preparation message.
- The system generates an IT request message.
- Messages include relevant employee details and next steps.
- Messages are drafts only and require human review.

Traceability: RF011, RF012, RF013.

#### PRD-P0-008: Final Onboarding Plan

User story: As an HR user, I want all outputs consolidated into a single plan so that I can review and coordinate onboarding from one place.

Acceptance criteria:

- The final plan includes profile, documents, IT checklist, training path, agenda, communications, pending actions, risks, and status.
- The plan can be exported or viewed as Markdown and structured JSON.
- The plan clearly identifies task owners where possible.
- The plan includes next recommended actions.

Traceability: RF010, RF014, RF015, RF017, RF020.

#### PRD-P0-009: Agent Action History

User story: As an HR manager, I want a history of agent actions so that generated outputs are auditable.

Acceptance criteria:

- The system records timestamp, agent name, action type, and generated result summary.
- The final plan includes or links to the action history.
- Errors or validation failures are recorded.

Traceability: RF018, MRD Production Requirements.

## 6. Non-Functional Requirements

### Performance

- MVP plan generation target: under 120 seconds for standard test cases.
- Input validation target: under 2 seconds.
- UI should remain responsive while agent execution runs.
- Agent execution should expose progress status where feasible.

### Reliability

- Failed specialist agent tasks must return a clear error and not silently disappear.
- The final plan must indicate incomplete sections if a specialist agent fails.
- The system must preserve submitted input even if generation fails.

### Security and Privacy

- Collect only data required for onboarding planning.
- Do not store sensitive documents in the MVP.
- Do not expose employee data in logs beyond necessary trace summaries.
- Protect API keys and model credentials through environment variables or secret storage.
- Treat uploaded or user-provided text as untrusted input.
- Require human approval before any future external action.

### Compliance and Governance

- The product must avoid employment scoring, ranking, disciplinary recommendations, promotion recommendations, termination recommendations, or automated eligibility decisions.
- The product must label AI-generated communications as drafts.
- Agent outputs must be reviewable before use.
- Action history must support auditability.
- Future production design should align with NIST AI RMF, OWASP LLM guidance, and ISO/IEC 42001 principles.

### Scalability

- MVP may run locally or in a lightweight hosted environment.
- Production architecture should support background job execution and queued onboarding workflows.
- Agent runs should be measurable by latency, token usage, cost, and validation success rate.

### Accessibility

- Web UI should target WCAG 2.1 AA practices where applicable.
- Form validation errors should be clear and accessible.
- Structured plan sections should be navigable by keyboard.

## 7. User Experience Design

### Interface Requirements

The MVP should be a workbench, not a marketing site.

Primary views:

- New collaborator intake form.
- Generation progress and agent activity.
- Consolidated onboarding plan.
- Pending actions and risks.
- Communication drafts.
- Export or copy-ready output.

The UI should be operational, compact, and easy to scan. HR users should be able to move quickly from input to generated plan to review.

### Agent Interaction Design

The system should show:

- Which agents contributed to the plan.
- What each agent produced.
- Which items are critical for first-day readiness.
- Which outputs need human review.
- Which assumptions were made due to missing data.

### Error Handling

The system should:

- Show validation errors before generation.
- Show partial output if one agent fails.
- Provide clear retry affordance.
- Mark incomplete sections explicitly.

## 8. Success Metrics & KPIs

### Product Metrics

- First onboarding plan generated successfully in MVP demo.
- At least 90% of P0 acceptance criteria passing in QA.
- 100% of required input fields validated.
- Final plan includes all required sections for complete sample input.
- Missing data scenario produces clear pending actions.

### Agent Quality Metrics

- Specialist output schema compliance rate.
- Percentage of checklist items grounded in local catalogs.
- Number of hallucinated or unsupported recommendations found during QA.
- Human acceptance rate for generated checklist items.
- Human acceptance rate for communication drafts.

### UX Metrics

- Time from completed input to reviewable plan.
- Number of manual edits required in demo scenarios.
- HR reviewer confidence score after reviewing generated plan.
- Task completion rate for primary MVP flow.

### Operational Metrics

- Agent run latency.
- Token usage per onboarding plan.
- Generation error rate.
- Retry rate.
- Log completeness rate.

| --- | --- |

## 10. Launch and Validation Strategy

### Beta Testing Plan

Initial beta should use simulated data and 3-5 representative scenarios:

- Remote software engineer.
- Hybrid sales representative.
- On-site operations employee.
- Finance or HR employee with compliance-heavy requirements.
- Manager or leadership role.

Each scenario should test:

- Required field validation.
- Role and department-specific recommendations.
- Work mode-specific IT needs.
- Training classification.
- Communication generation.
- Pending action and risk detection.

### MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is successful when:

1. HR can enter required collaborator data.
2. Missing data is detected and shown clearly.
3. Specialist agents generate their expected outputs.
4. The coordinator consolidates outputs into a complete plan.
5. The plan includes documents, IT tasks, training, communications, status, risks, and next actions.
6. The flow visibly demonstrates multi-agent collaboration.
7. Generated content remains draft/reviewable and does not perform external actions.

### Go-To-Market Direction

For this project stage, go-to-market is secondary to proving the MVP. The product should be demoed as:

- An internal AI workflow prototype for HR operations.
- A lightweight onboarding orchestration proof of concept.
- A foundation for future HRIS/ITSM/IAM integrations.

Potential commercial model later:

- SaaS pricing by active onboarding volume.
- Team pricing for HR/People Ops.
- Enterprise pilot fee for integrations and governance.
